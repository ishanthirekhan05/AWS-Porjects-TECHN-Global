Resume essentials

A clearer resume, section by section

Keep every line accurate and relevant.

01

Resume-building tips

Shape your resume around the kind of role or internship you are learning toward.

  • Keep the layout simple, consistent, and easy to scan.
  • Use clear action verbs to describe your contribution.
  • Prioritize relevant skills, projects, and coursework.
  • Proofread names, dates, links, and contact details.
02

ATS-friendly guidance

An applicant tracking system may parse resume text before a person reviews it. Make the content predictable and readable.

  • Use standard headings such as Education, Skills, and Projects.
  • Match truthful keywords from the role description.
  • Prefer normal text over text inside images, shapes, or headers.
  • Use common fonts, straightforward dates, and a clean file name.

Reminder: ATS guidance improves readability; it does not guarantee selection.

03

Important resume sections

Choose sections that give a complete and honest view of your current experience.

  • Contact details and a professional profile link
  • Short objective or summary, when useful
  • Education and relevant coursework
  • Technical and professional skills
  • Projects, internships, certifications, or experience
04

Common resume mistakes

Small inconsistencies can make strong work harder to evaluate.

  • Using one identical resume for every opportunity
  • Listing tools without showing how you used them
  • Including unverified claims, copied text, or inflated results
  • Leaving broken links, spelling errors, or inconsistent formatting
  • Adding personal information that is not relevant to the role
05

How to describe projects

Give a reviewer enough context to understand the problem, your decisions, and what you learned.

Project context

State what you built and the user or problem it addressed.

Your contribution

Identify the parts you designed, implemented, tested, or documented.

Technology choices

Name the tools and briefly connect each one to its purpose.

Evidence and learning

Share a truthful result, link, or lesson without inventing metrics.
